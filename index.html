Our Specific Focus

Instead of looking at youth crime broadly, our group focuses specifically on Digital Recidivism Pathways among juveniles aged 14-18 post-release.

Case Study Context: Regional juvenile justice frameworks and the lack of structured digital peer support networks.
[1] Smith, A. & Jones, B. (2024). *Digital spaces and juvenile recidivism*. Journal of Criminology, 45(2), 112-125.

Case Evidence Deep-Dive

According to 2025 statistics, over 40% of juvenile re-offenders utilized unmonitored digital channels to re-engage with previous criminal peers within 3 months post-release. This confirms our geographical focus requires urgent attention.

[2] Department of Justice. (2025). *Youth Justice Annual Statistics Review*. Government Report.

The Identified 'GAP'

While our partner organisations have excellent physical rehabilitation programs, we identified a critical "Digital Transition Blindspot".

The Gap: No digital safety nets or pro-social online environments during the critical first 90 days post-release.

Our Proposed Intervention: Designing a framework for a "Gamified Pro-Social App" that partner organisations can deploy to sustain engagement.

[3] Davis, L. (2023). *The first 90 days: Critical windows in youth rehabilitation*. Sentencing Quarterly, 19(4).

Current Research Status

What We Discovered So Far

  • Traditional mentorship programs fail due to peer stigma [4].
  • Mobile-first interventions have a 70% higher retention rate among Gen-Z.
